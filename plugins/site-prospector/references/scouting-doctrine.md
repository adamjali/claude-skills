# Demo-Site Scouting Doctrine v1
state-wide, minus the two largest metros (they are saturated and out of reach) · Rural weighted UP · Built and pitched 100% online.
Authored by Fable 5 (2026-07-23), verified against live state-data sources. This is the canonical scouting reference; `candidate-search.md` is the older, narrower rubric it supersedes.

**Honest framing:** our 16 demos prove we can find owners who REPLY (a gutter client replied instantly, a landscaping client called in). They do NOT yet prove which verticals BUY. Score two things separately: "will they SEE the pitch" and "can/will they PAY monthly." Vertical fit ratings are grounded hypotheses, not closed cases. Baseline: ~27-29% of US small businesses still have no website, concentrated in exactly our profile (older owners, trades, rural, "social media is enough"). Revise this doctrine on Won/Lost evidence, don't defend it.

## STEP 0) THE CANDIDATE-SHAPE TIERS (priority order, NOT a hard gate)

Review count is free, visible without a click, and proxies two things at once: "is this
real" and "has anyone got here first." Use it to **order the work queue**, not to delete
candidates. Nothing below is excluded; lower tiers are simply worked after higher ones.
Full reasoning in 2b. Confirmed by live pipeline run 2026-07-27 (see 2d).

### TIER 1 — PRIMARY. Work these first, always.
| Shape | Rank within tier |
|---|---|
| 5-25 Google reviews + **no website** | good |
| ≤20 reviews + website that **does not work** | better (budget decision already made) |
| ≤20 reviews + website that **looks bad** | best (decision made, budget proven, gap visible in one second) |

Why this is primary: reviews prove real paying customers without any research, which clears
G2 on sight, and the 5-25 band means nobody competent has worked them yet. Over ~25 means
they are already being courted.

### TIER 2 — LOW/ZERO REVIEWS. Still workable, worked after Tier 1.
**0-4 reviews is NOT a disqualifier.** It usually means new, dormant, or simply never asked
a customer for a review, and the third case is common among exactly our profile (older
owners, rural trades, phone-only operations). The cost is that "is this real" and "will they
pay" both become unproven, so verification work goes UP and confidence goes DOWN. Score them
normally; they will naturally land lower because Factor B (pulse) and Factor F (pay) have
less evidence behind them. Do not skip them, and do not fabricate confidence about them.

### TIER 3 — NO the state corporate registry REGISTRATION AT ALL. Workable, lowest priority.
**A business does not need an LLC or a the state corporate registry record to be a real business or a real
customer.** Sole proprietors, cash trades, and informal operators are a large share of the
rural/older-owner profile this doctrine targets, and they are invisible to every
registration-based feed. Find them through the community sources in section 3 (Facebook
groups, Marketplace, Nextdoor, Maps, fair and market vendor lists, church bulletins,
shopper weeklies, Craigslist).

For Tier 3, G2 ("real business") is satisfied by **evidence of customers**, not by a filing:
recent reviews, dated job photos, customer comments, an active listing, a working phone that
a human answers. See the amended G2 note below. Expect higher effort per candidate and
weaker pay signals; that is why they are last, not why they are excluded.

**Order of operations: tier the queue → G1-G4 gates → 0-100 score → bands.**
The tiers set WHAT ORDER you work. The gates and score still decide who gets built.

## 2) SCOUTING RUBRIC (score 0-100 after gates)

**Gates (pass/fail, ALL required):**
- **G1 No real site** — verified at SCOUT and RE-VERIFIED at BUILD (the one owner / newtonsenterprises lesson). FB-only, parked, broken, or genuinely outdated PASSES. A decent modern site fails. **DNS alone is NOT enough, and neither is a search result or an HTTP status (amended 2026-07-28 after B & C Nursery, High Maintenance Tree, and The Rusty Hook all slipped through with live sites).** The method: generate domain PERMUTATIONS from the name (`&`→"and", joined words, initials, first+last word, `+<state>`, across .com/.net) → `dig +short` each → FETCH every hit → strip `<script>`/`<style>` → read the VISIBLE TEXT and classify on content (parked / for-sale / coming-soon / 404 language vs real nav like Services, Contact, Estimate). A 200 response with a megabyte of HTML is routinely a parked lander. `business.site` links are always dead (Google killed the product in 2024) so those PASS. Full detail in 2d.
- **G2 Real business** — customer activity within ~6 months. Active the state corporate registry/the state licensing board is the cleanest proof but is **sufficient, not necessary** (amended 2026-07-27): an unregistered sole proprietor is still a real business and a real customer. Where there is no filing, satisfy G2 with evidence of CUSTOMERS instead — recent reviews, dated job photos, customer comments, an active listing, a phone a human answers. Primary records over aggregator copy either way.
- **G3 Reachable** — at least one working channel (phone, FB Messenger, Nextdoor, IG, email).
- **G4 Clean record** — no unlicensed-in-a-licensed-trade, no BBB-F pattern. Extra caution in UNLICENSED verticals (ST hunting guides): nothing official to check, so reputation evidence must be stronger.

**Score (0-100). Rebalanced 2026-07-28 — see 2e for why the old weights were wrong.**

The old rubric put 50 of 100 points on A(25)+B(25), which rewarded being NEW and LOUD and
systematically buried established Tier 1 operators. A is now a timing bonus rather than half
the score, and **Factor G (proven demand)** gives established businesses a way to earn points
for the thing that actually makes them good customers.

| # | Factor | Max | Scoring |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Newness / timing | **15** | the state corporate registry filing or opening <6mo=15 · 6-18mo=11 · 18mo-3yr=7 · >3yr=**5 (neutral floor, NOT a penalty)** · **>10yr with no GBP and 0-2 reviews = 12 (an EARNED bonus, not a floor — see 2d)** |
| B | Owner personal pulse | **20** | Owner's OWN post/reply (customer reviews count ZERO): <7d=20 · <30d=15 · <90d=9 · <6mo=4 · none findable=0. **If not checked, record `B?(unverified)` and mark the total provisional — never score an unchecked B as 0.** |
| C | Presence-gap severity | 20 | Nothing/unclaimed GBP=20 · **domain bought but unfinished/"coming soon"=18** · parked/expired domain=18 · FB-page-only=16 · broken=14 · directory-listings-only=16 · ugly/outdated real site=8 · semi-functional builder=4 · decent site=fails G1 |
| D | Older / non-tech owner | 10 | 20+yr tenure zero web, "call only," landline, hand-painted signage, all-caps FB, no booking apps. A young digitally-fluent owner scores 0, and that is correct: they are harder to impress and likelier to DIY. |
| E | Rural | 10 | Rural small town=10 · exurban/small city=6 · metro=0 |
| F | Can + will pay | 10 | Big-ticket trade (roofing, barns, docks, charters, tree, cabinets) 9-10 · mid-ticket recurring (lawn, pools, grooming, cleaning, pressure wash) 6-8 · gig-scale 0-3. **Already paying for ANY presence (domain, Squarespace, Thumbtack, boosted posts, chamber dues, vehicle wrap) maxes this factor.** |
| G | **Proven demand (NEW)** | **15** | **Score the HIGHER of two paths, never add them (corrected 2026-07-29).**<br>**G1 EFFORT (the stronger predictor):** owner is actively selling on a platform he does not own. Posts his own pitch into local FB groups / Nextdoor threads / replies to "anyone recommend a..." within 7d=**15** · within 30d=12 · pays a lead marketplace (GreenPal, Angi, Thumbtack, LawnStarter) or runs flyers/coupons/boosted posts=12 · directory listing he clearly maintains=8 · nothing=0.<br>**G2 REVIEWS:** 20-25=15 · 10-19=12 · 5-9=9 · 1-4=5 · 0 but customer evidence exists (job photos, neighbour comments)=3 · nothing=0. **Cap at 25**; above that they are being courted and C/F should reflect it.<br>**Why the change:** all 5 converting leads had **0-1 reviews** (a landscaping client: trading since 1998, ONE review, from 2013; a junk-removal client: zero). Scoring G on reviews alone gave the best lead in the pipeline a 3/15. **Effort beats reviews as a demand signal here** (see 2d). |

**Why G exists:** an established operator with 24 reviews and no website is a BETTER demo-site
customer than a five-day-old LLC with no customers, because demand and money are already
proven. The old rubric scored that backwards. G is deliberately capped at 15 so it balances
A's timing bonus rather than replacing it.

**Bands:** 75+ build now · 60-74 shortlist (build when 75+ pool empty) · 45-59 monitor, re-score monthly (pulse decays fast) · <45 pass, log the reason in the tracker so we never re-scout it.

Reading it honestly: A+B (50 pts) predict "will they SEE the pitch and act." C+D+E predict "is the gap real and undefended." F + pay modifiers predict "will they PAY," the part with the least evidence. A reply is not a sale (a gutter client). On ties, take the higher F.

## 2d) THE CONVERTING-LEAD PROFILE (added 2026-07-29, from 5 interested vs 19 declined)

Derived from the live tracker: 79 logged calls, 5 leads that showed real interest (a landscaping client, a lawn-care client, a small-engine client, a junk-removal client, a lawn-care client) against 19 that declined. **This outranks every other targeting heuristic in this file when they conflict.**

> **The profile: an established trade, often 10+ years old, working hard for leads on Nextdoor / Facebook community groups / GreenPal, with ZERO or ONE real review, NO Google Business Profile, and either no website or a domain they still own that went dark.**
>
> Not "no online presence." **Maximum effort, zero owned assets.**

**The six shared attributes:**
1. **No Google Business Profile.** Confirmed on 3 of 5. a junk-removal client Google Places returns only his competitors; he is invisible on the surface that matters.
2. **0-1 real reviews despite decades trading.** a landscaping client: operating since **1998**, has **one** review, from **2013**. a junk-removal client: **zero** attributable reviews (twice verified).
3. **Established, not new.** 28 / 23 / 11 years for three of five.
4. **Hustling on rented land.** a junk-removal client posts into six FB groups and hit Nextdoor 3x in one hour. a lawn-care client runs flyers + coupon + WhatsApp + GreenPal. **This is the strongest single tell and it is what Factor G should be scoring.**
5. **A dead domain they still own.** 2 of 5 (a junk-removal client expired; a lawn-care client dark since Dec 2021, paid through 2028). They already decided once to buy a site.
6. **Outdoor recurring home services** (lawn, landscaping, junk, tree) = 4 of 5.

**What did NOT predict interest, so stop filtering on it:**
- **Entity status.** a lawn-care client was admin-dissolved; **a junk-removal client has no LLC at all** and is the most engaged lead in the pipeline. the state corporate registry standing predicted nothing about interest (it still matters for what you may CLAIM on a build, per G4 and the truth rules).
- **The 5-25 review band in 2b.** These sit at 0-1. That band belongs to the paid-agency play, not the demo-site play. **When 2b and this section conflict, this section wins for demo-site scouting.**
- **Newness.** See above; new filings answer with AI screeners.
- **Ability to pay as a pre-filter.** Two cited money and still engaged. Money is a closing problem here, not a targeting one.

**Where to find them.** The trap: **you cannot find these people on Google, because their absence from Google IS the gap.** Hunt where they are:
1. **Nextdoor, hardest.** Neighbours asking "anyone recommend a..." and the operators replying in-thread. This is how a junk-removal client was found.
2. **Local FB community groups** (town-name Scene / Chat / Community pages, one per suburb). Same channel surfaced Jp's Lawn and ALL SCREENS on 2026-07-29.
3. **Lead marketplaces** (GreenPal, Angi, Thumbtack, LawnStarter): paying for leads with no owned site = pay-proven and asset-poor.
4. **Expired/parked domains** cross-referenced against active operators (play #2 in section 5, badly under-used).
5. **Google Maps in reverse:** search trade + city, then find who is visible on Nextdoor/Yelp but **absent from the map pack entirely.**

**The pitch it implies** (not "you don't have a website"):
> *"You're working hard for leads on Nextdoor and in the Facebook groups, and every bit of it lives on someone else's platform. You don't show up on Google at all, and you have one review from 2013."*

**Caveat:** 5 of 24 conversations. The no-GBP and rented-platform signals are weighted highest because they have a mechanism, not just a correlation. Reach remains a separate bottleneck (56 of 79 calls no-answer), so one no-answer is never grounds to mark a lead Lost.

## 2b) THE REVIEW-COUNT BAND + WHO CAN ACTUALLY PAY (added 2026-07-27)

From two operators running the paid-agency version of this at volume (Kai Stone, ~1,300 clients at $200-500/mo; Santrel Media). Both are affiliate-monetized (see the affiliate note at the end of this section), so treat tool recommendations as paid and the targeting criteria as the useful part.

**The Google-review band is the fastest single filter, and it is new to this doctrine.** Kai's VAs build lists on exactly three shapes:

| Shape | Signal |
|---|---|
| **5-25 Google reviews + NO website** | The core target. Enough reviews to prove a real operating business with real customers (clears G2 without research), few enough that nobody competent has worked them. |
| **≤20 reviews + website that doesn't work** | Better. They already decided once to have a site, same logic as the parked-domain play. |
| **≤20 reviews + website that looks bad** | Best of the three, in his words. Decision already made, budget already proven, gap self-evident on screen. |

**Why the band beats a raw no-website scan:** review count is a free, visible proxy for both "is this real" (G2) and "has anyone got here first." Under ~5 reviews often means too new or too dormant to pay. Over ~25 means they are being actively courted, or already have someone. **Add review count as a pre-filter before scoring, not as a scoring factor**, it cheaply removes most of the list before a human looks at anything.

**The margin filter, and it is price-point dependent.** Santrel's warning: do NOT lead with restaurants and food businesses for a paid retainer, because their margins run 3-4% and they genuinely do not have $1,000/mo for marketing. He recommends dentists, doctors, lawyers, CPAs, accountants, trades where one new customer is worth $1,000-5,000, so they can spend to acquire.

**This does not overturn the LEAD tier above, and the reason matters.** Our food-vertical placement is for the **$79-99/mo demo-site offer**, which a thin-margin restaurant can absolutely afford. Santrel is describing a **$1,000/mo package**, which they cannot. Both are correct at their own price point. The rule to carry forward:

> **Match the vertical to the price of the offer, not to the size of the gap.** A 3% margin business is a fine $79/mo customer and a terrible $499/mo customer. Screen for margin only once the offer crosses roughly $300/mo.

Note also that our AVOID tier already excludes lawyers/realtors/insurance for *our* offer (saturated, corporate, ad rules), which is the opposite of Santrel's recommendation. That divergence is deliberate and stays: he is selling generic paid-ads retainers nationally; we are selling local presence to under-served owners in my state. Do not "fix" this to match him.

**Already-paying-for-marketing stays the strongest pay signal** (existing Factor F). Kai's independent version of the same rule: only pitch businesses already spending on Google Ads, Meta ads, Thumbtack or similar. It proves willingness to spend, which nothing else does.

## 2c) WHERE TO HIRE THE LIST-BUILDERS (added 2026-07-27)

**Do not use scrapers to build the call list.** Kai names Outscraper and ScrapeGod and rejects both: dead numbers, closed businesses, and dirty data you then pay for in wasted dials. Hand-built beats scraped here because the filter criteria above (does the site look bad? does it load?) need a human eye.

**Hire a VA to build it manually.** Reported economics: **$3-4/hour**, roughly **$5 per 100 leads**, **$10 per 200**. Ten hours of VA time produces more list than you can work through in months.

**Where:**
- **OnlineJobs.ph**: direct hire, Philippines, no middleman or agency margin, 160,000+ VAs listed. This is the standard route for the $3-5/hr price point. You handle payment, time tracking and onboarding yourself.
- **Upwork**: the general marketplace. Faster to start, higher rates, escrow and dispute handling included.
- **Paired**: named by Santrel as how they hired a full-time remote employee (theirs is in Ecuador). Agency-style placement, higher cost, less setup work.

**The brief to give them** is the three shapes in 2b plus the county or city list, output to a spreadsheet with a notes column so you can mark where you stopped calling. Nothing more complicated than that.

**Second use for the same hire:** project-based site builds. Kai paid VAs roughly **$40 per website build**, and only after a client had closed, so the cost only ever lands against revenue. Worth considering once volume makes hand-building the constraint.

**Standing note on the sources: every operator recommending GoHighLevel in this research is a paid GHL affiliate.** Kai Stone and Nick Ponte both state it on camera; Juan and Noah both push affiliate links. Santrel is monetized on Hostinger and Northwest Registered Agent instead. There is currently **zero disinterested testimony** in favour of any tool named in these videos. Their *targeting* and *sales* observations are worth far more than their tool picks.

## 2d) THE CROSS-REFERENCE PLAY + WHAT THE LIVE RUN ACTUALLY SHOWED (added 2026-07-27)

Run: the state corporate registry daily SFTP, corp + fictitious feeds, filings 2026-07-25 to 07-27. 421 GO-FIRST
vertical matches → 388 after excluding the two largest metros → 137 with a usable real address →
51 rural / 29 exurban. Artifacts in `~/money/scout-2026-07-27/`.

**Finding 1 — Tier 1 and the the state corporate registry-daily play are in tension, by construction.**
An LLC filed five days ago has zero Google reviews and no six-month customer history, so a
brand-new filing can never satisfy Tier 1 and mostly fails G2 as originally written. Tested,
not assumed: of the first five rural candidates checked, **four had no findable web presence
at all** (searches returned their competitors). This is why STEP 0 is now tiers, not a gate,
and why G2 accepts customer evidence in place of a filing.

**Finding 2 — the productive shape is the INTERSECTION, and it beats either feed alone.**
The fifth candidate had a Yelp and Nextdoor presence: an operator who had been running
informally for a while and only now incorporated. Sampling ten new filings, roughly **3 in
10 are existing operators formalizing** rather than true startups. Those clear every gate at
once: proven customers, real reviews, usually still no website (the person who never bothered
with an LLC never bothered with a site), and they are at a moment of active investment in the
business.

> **The play: use the the state corporate registry feed as a NAME SOURCE, then cross-reference each name against
> Google / Yelp / Nextdoor / Facebook. New filing + pre-existing customer history + no site
> is a sharper target than either the raw new-filing list or the raw review-band list.**

Keep BOTH raw lanes running as well (2026-07-27 decision, do not narrow to only the
intersection): the new-filing lane still wins the "reach them before they buy a Wix" race,
and the review-band lane still finds established operators who never registered anything.

**Finding 3 — registered-agent addresses poison geography.** 17 of 388 listed a registered-agent
service address (Northwest Registered Agent, 7901 4th St N, a coastal city **33702**) as their
*principal* address. Their real location is unknowable from the filing, so Factor E cannot be
scored on it. Detect and exclude from geo-scoring rather than recording them as the coastal county
businesses. Other RA services to watch: Registered Agents Inc, LegalZoom, Incfile, ZenBusiness,
Harbor Compliance, Cogency, CT Corporation, Corporation Service Company, Spiegel & Utrera.

**Finding 4 — the same-name trap fires on this feed too.** "SORIANO LANDSCAPE LLC" (Bowling
Green, ST) matched a "Soriano Landscaping" with reviews — in Palm Desert and Jacumba Hot
Springs, **California**, with a 424 area code. Common surname, different business, different
coast. Confirm state and area code before crediting any presence found by name search.

**Tooling note:** the the state corporate registry SFTP needs a password-capable client. This Mac has `sftp` but no
`sshpass`, and its `curl` is not built with SFTP. Use Python `paramiko` (`pip install paramiko`).
Record layout: bytes 0-11 doc number, 12-203 entity name, then principal address; locate
city/state/zip with the regex `([A-Z][A-Z .\'-]+?)\s{2,}<ST>(\d{5})\s+US(\d{8})` where the
trailing 8 digits are the MMDDYYYY filing date.

## 2e) RUBRIC REBALANCE — RESOLVED (2026-07-28)

**The bug, found by running the Tier 1 lane for the first time:** 50 of 100 points (A newness
25 + B pulse 25) rewarded being new and loud. A Tier 1 candidate is neither by definition, so
established operators with 17-24 reviews and no website scored **~46** ("monitor monthly")
while a five-day-old LLC with zero customers scored **~80**. Exactly backwards, and STEP 0
already names Tier 1 as PRIMARY.

**The fix, applied to section 2:** A 25→15, B 25→20, and a new **Factor G Proven demand (15)**
scored on review count. A's floor is now explicitly neutral, not a penalty. Nothing was dropped
from the pipeline; only the ordering changed.

**Effect on the live 2026-07-27 candidates:** Tier 1 operators moved from ~46 into the 57-73
range, and the top new-filing lead (Savage Actual: proven demand + an unfinished paid-for
domain + already spending) correctly rose to the top at ~81. Ordering now matches the doctrine's
own stated priority.

**Two reading rules that stay permanent:**
1. **B=0 means UNVERIFIED, not absent.** Record `B?(unverified)`, treat the total as provisional,
   and never report an unverified-B score as final.
2. **A=5 is neutral.** An established business is a better customer, not a worse one.

## 5) STRATEGIC PLAYS
1. **the state corporate registry daily pipeline** (verified) — daily filings + fictitious-name/DBA feed off public SFTP. A 3-week-old LLC or fresh DBA has no site, is in buying mode, nobody else is pitching it. The DBA feed is what competitors miss (catches rural sole proprietors).
2. **Parked-domain hunt** — a parked/expired domain means the owner already decided once to have a site. Cross-reference parked domains vs active the state corporate registry names by county = highest-intent list (we caught example-business.com incidentally).
3. **the state licensing board new-licensee scan** — newness + legitimacy in one weekly file (contractors, barbers, cosmo, inspectors, food trucks).
4. **Ownership-transition moments** — the state corporate registry officer changes, "under new management." New owner modernizes everything at once (+5).
5. **Storm-wave scouting** — after a named storm, tree/tarp/haul/fence/roof crews surge + new LLCs file within days. Pre-built checklist, move inside a week.
6. **Seasonality (pitch 4-8 weeks pre-season):** Jan tax/bookkeeping · Feb-Apr pools/lawn ramp · May hurricane prep · Aug-Sep hunting (processors/taxidermy/guides) · Oct-Nov snowbirds (home watch/cleaning/RV parks) + holiday lights · Nov-Jan wedding-venue booking.
7. **Pay-proven proxies beat demographics** — anyone paying for presence anywhere (Thumbtack, boosted posts, bulletin ads, chamber dues, a wrap) has demonstrated the exact behavior we sell.
8. **Bilingual one-pagers** — English+Spanish single pages for taquerias, landscaping crews, cleaning services. Nobody local offers this at our price.
9. **Phone-first everything** — rural/older owners only ever see the demo on a phone via text or Messenger link. Mobile is the pitch surface.
10. **Honest self-assessment, standing** — reply rate proven, purchase rate not. One build per unproven vertical until one converts; tracker Won/Lost feeds back into fit ratings.

**Tactics the operator videos teach that we DO NOT run (added 2026-07-28, each has real enforcement behind it):**
- **Fake or extra Google Business Profiles.** FTC + Illinois sued Premium Home Service (May 2026) for exactly this: thousands of fake local listings with local addresses and local phone numbers. Also self-punishing, since suspensions kill the asset.
- **Review gating** (screening sentiment, only sending happy customers the link). FTC 16 CFR 465, in force 2024-10-21, max $53,088 per violation. A major retailer paid $4.2m in 2022 for blocking sub-4-star reviews. Google's content guidelines separately ban "selectively soliciting positive reviews."
- **Incentivising CUSTOMERS for reviews**, including charity-donation-per-review. No charity exception exists. Paying your own tech a $25-50 bonus is fine; paying the customer is not.
- **Writing reviews or community posts as a customer.** One source recommends posting in local FB groups "from a customer's point of view as the business owner." Astroturfing, same FTC rule. Asking a happy customer to recommend you is fine; writing it yourself is not.
- **Citation volume claims** ("50+ citations"). Value comes from NAP consistency, so an inconsistent citation is worse than none. Ship 10-15 accurate plus cleanup. This is the exact claim the earlier adversarial critique caught us on.
- **Self-ranking listicles placed off-site.** "Best X in [city]" on the client's own blog with them at #1 is transparent marketing. The same article on Medium or as a guest post, without disclosing the #1 company wrote it, is the LendEDU fact pattern the FTC acted on.
- **Doorway pages.** Mass near-duplicate city pages. Test: strip the template, and if under ~30-40% of what remains is unique, don't ship it.

Full ranking method, including everything we DO run: `~/.claude/explanations/20260724_valstratis-replication/SEO-PLAYBOOK.md`.

**Standing truth rules:** re-verify no-site at BUILD via live DNS · never fabricate reviews/stars/counts (thin-review → facts-only ledger; one real review → feature + attribute) · adversarial verification before every build · not-affiliated disclaimer on every demo · primary records (the state corporate registry, the state licensing board, the state wildlife/marine licence registry, FDACS) over aggregator copy · in unlicensed verticals demand stronger reputation evidence.

## 2e. THE CLOSED-WON PRINCIPLE (added 2026-07-29)

**Optimize on the outcome you are paid for, never on the proxy that is easy to measure.**

This is the rule that produced section 2d, and it is worth naming because it keeps
reasserting itself. Borrowed from paid-advertising attribution, where the failure mode is
textbook: tell an ad platform to optimize for **form fills** and it finds people who fill
out forms, a genuinely different population from people who buy. Tell it to optimize for
**closed-won** (deals that actually paid) and it finds buyers. Full explanation, vocabulary
and the reason this operation does NOT sell paid ads: `paid-ads-and-attribution.md`.

**Applied to scouting:**

- **Judge a lead SOURCE by the deals it closes, not the leads it produces.** Nextdoor and
  local Facebook groups earn their place because a junk-removal client, a lawn-care client, a lawn-care client and
  a landscaping client came from there. Not because they are easy to search.
- **Judge a SCORING FACTOR by whether it correlates with conversion.** Factor G scored
  proven demand by review count and therefore gave a junk-removal client, the most engaged lead in the
  pipeline, a 3/15. The factor was measuring a proxy (reviews) instead of the outcome
  (engagement). It was rewritten on 5-interested-vs-19-declined evidence.
- **Re-run the analysis as the sample grows.** 5 conversions is a thin sample. The profile
  in 2d is the best current read, not a law. When the interested count reaches ~15, redo
  it from the call log and expect at least one current belief to break.
- **Proxies to distrust by default:** review count, entity/the state corporate registry status, business age,
  "looks professional", number of calls made, number of leads logged. Every one of these
  has already misled this operation at least once.

## 2f. THE DEMO-QUALIFICATION GATE (added 2026-07-30, from the Liquid Agency teardown)

**Before building any spec demo, fetch the prospect's CURRENT site and count the words.**
This is a hard gate, not a scoring factor, and it protects the single most expensive thing we do.

**The evidence.** Liquid Agency (liquidagency.eu, Milan, solo operator) publishes 6 demo previews.
Five are distinct businesses. Conversion was verified from outside by fetching each demo, resolving
the client's real domain, and comparing `<title>` + hosting fingerprint:

| Demo | Client's incumbent site at pitch time | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Gian Luca Maglio | weak/none | ✅ **converted** — gianlucamaglio.it, Vercel, identical title |
| E85 | weak/none | ✅ **converted** — e85.it, Vercel, identical title |
| Foto Furlan | weak/none | ✅ **converted** — fotofurlan.it, identical title |
| Valerio Di Domenica | **2,080-word live site** | ❌ kept his own |
| Marco Biasini | **8,938-word Wix site** | ❌ **still on Wix after TWO demos were built for him** |

**3 of 5 = ~60% conversion — and the two failures are perfectly predicted by one variable: the
prospect already had a substantial incumbent site.**

**The gate:**
- **No site · dead site · parked domain · under ~500 words → demo is a strong play.** Build it.
- **~500-1,000 words → judge case by case.** Look at whether it is actually maintained (dated
  content, working forms, current phone).
- **1,000+ words on a live, maintained site → DO NOT SPEC-BUILD.** Pitch a rebuild conversation,
  or skip. A prospect with a 8,900-word Wix site is not a demo target no matter how dated it looks.
  Liquid proved building it twice does not change the answer.

**Why this holds.** It is the same finding as §2d from our own 79 calls, reproduced independently
in another country and another language: **the free-demo play wins against no-site and dead-site
prospects and loses against incumbents.** Two datasets, same conclusion. Treat it as settled.

**Operationally:** the word count is already produced by the fetch-and-read verification in §2d —
just record it and apply the threshold. It costs nothing extra.
