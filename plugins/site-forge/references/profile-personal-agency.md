## Who it serves

Two audiences at once, which is the whole difficulty:

1. **Prospects for the demo-site operation** — local trade owners who need to believe
   this person builds serious websites and will answer the phone. They judge on proof
   and plainness.
2. **Peers, collaborators and anyone assessing credibility** — who judge on taste.

A site that only does (1) reads like a template vendor. A site that only does (2) fails
to convert the people who actually pay. The tension between those is the design problem,
and it is why this cannot just inherit `creative-portfolio`.

## Bends: to be determined

Do not populate these from taste. Populate them from the brief when Adam gives it, and
from a teardown of sites in the actual competitive set (independent web studios and
solo operators, not photographers).

Open questions to resolve with Adam before building:

- Does this present as **a person** or **a studio**? It changes the whole voice, and the
  current build hedges.
- Does it show the demo fleet as **case studies with outcomes**, or as **a gallery**?
  Case studies convert trades; a gallery reads better. Probably both, separated.
- How much of the operation is disclosed? Pricing publicly, or on a call?
- Does it carry the `creative-portfolio` type scale, or stay closer to the default? The
  audience is split, so this is a genuine decision rather than an obvious one.
